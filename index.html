About Me

I am an applied environmental and community economist and a Ph.D. candidate in Agricultural Economics at Purdue University. My research examines how environmental risks and policies shape household decisions and local economic outcomes. I am particularly interested in how environmental change affects communities through housing markets, residential sorting, and the distribution of economic impacts across households.

My dissertation examines how flood risk and flood buyout programs affect residential decisions and neighborhood housing markets. In my job market paper, I study how exposure to flood buyouts affects neighborhood housing values and how these effects vary across households and communities using a general equilibrium residential sorting model. My other research examines the economic value of environmental quality and ecosystem services, including water quality improvements, recreation, and wildlife management.

I am on the job market in the 2026–2027 academic year.
