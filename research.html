Research

My research lies at the intersection of environmental and resource economics, public economics, and community development. I study how environmental risks and policies shape household decisions and local economic outcomes, with a particular focus on housing markets, residential choice, climate adaptation, and the distribution of environmental impacts across households.

Job Market Paper

The Economic Effects of Voluntary and Mandatory Flood Buyout Programs: Evidence from an Equilibrium Sorting Model

How do flood buyout programs affect housing markets and residential decisions? This paper develops a general equilibrium residential sorting model to examine how households value neighborhoods affected by voluntary and mandatory flood buyouts and how these preferences differ across household groups. The model allows me to study how buyout programs affect residential sorting and neighborhood housing markets while accounting for heterogeneous preferences across households.

Progress: Structural model estimated; specification and inference in development.

Other Research

Property Value Impacts of Flood Buyouts: Checkerboard Effects, Flood Risk, and Green Space

This paper examines how flood buyout activity is capitalized into nearby property values. I study whether the effects of buyouts vary by buyout type, proximity, and the spatial pattern of acquisitions, with particular attention to the potential differences between voluntary and mandatory buyouts.

Willingness to Pay for Sandhill Crane Hunting

This research examines preferences and willingness to pay for recreational hunting opportunities, contributing to broader work on recreation demand, wildlife management, and the economic value of environmental amenities.
