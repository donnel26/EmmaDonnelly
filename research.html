Research

My research lies at the intersection of environmental and resource economics, housing and residential choice, and community economic development. I study how environmental risks and policies shape household decisions, housing markets, and local communities, with a particular interest in climate adaptation, managed retreat, and the distribution of environmental and economic impacts across households. My work uses applied econometric, nonmarket valuation, and structural methods to study these questions.

Dissertation: Flood Buyouts, Housing Markets, Residential Sorting, and Welfare

My dissertation examines the economic effects of flood buyout programs on housing markets, residential sorting, and household welfare. I study the Harris County Flood Buyout Program in Texas, focusing on how voluntary and mandatory buyouts affect property values, household residential choices, neighborhood composition, and the distribution of economic impacts across households. The research program combines housing-market capitalization, residential sorting, and equilibrium analysis to examine how managed retreat changes both the value of residential locations and the households who choose to live in them.

1. Housing-Market Capitalization

How do flood buyouts affect housing values?

Property Value Effects of Flood Buyouts

My first dissertation paper examines how voluntary and mandatory flood buyouts are capitalized into nearby property values. Using property transaction data, I estimate how housing values vary with proximity to and exposure to buyout activity. I examine whether these effects differ by buyout type, distance from buyout properties, and the intensity of nearby buyout activity. This analysis provides evidence on how housing markets respond to the neighborhood changes associated with managed retreat.

Progress: Hedonic estimates completed; currently developing the paper for submission.

2. Residential Sorting and Heterogeneous Preferences

How do flood buyouts affect where people choose to live?

The Economic Effects of a Voluntary and Mandatory Flood Buyout Program: Evidence from an Equilibrium Sorting Model.

My second dissertation paper examines how households value neighborhoods affected by flood buyouts and how these preferences differ across household groups. I estimate a general equilibrium residential sorting model that allows preferences for voluntary and mandatory buyout exposure to vary across income and race/ethnicity. The model allows me to estimate household preferences and willingness to pay for buyout exposure, while examining how heterogeneous preferences shape residential sorting across neighborhoods. Treating voluntary and mandatory buyouts as distinct neighborhood characteristics also allows me to examine whether households respond differently to different forms of managed retreat.

Progress: Structural model estimation in progress; paper in progress; preliminary results available.

What are the broader equilibrium effects of flood buyouts?

I will extend the residential sorting analysis to examine how flood buyouts affect household relocation, neighborhood composition, housing demand, and welfare in equilibrium.

How I Use Residential Sorting Models

In my dissertation, I use a residential sorting model to estimate how households with different characteristics value neighborhood characteristics, including exposure to flood buyouts. The model connects observed residential choices to household preferences and allows me to study how changes in neighborhood characteristics affect where households choose to live, housing demand, and welfare.

Below is a simplified example using made-up data. The example includes a main script, which sets up the data and estimation, and a separate log-likelihood function, which calculates choice probabilities, matches predicted and observed market shares using the BLP contraction mapping, and estimates the model parameters.

Other Research

My broader research examines the economic value of environmental quality and ecosystem services, as well as how environmental policies affect household behavior and resource use.

Environmental Quality and Residential Mobility

My published research examines how improvements in water quality affect residential mobility and property-related welfare. This work uses revealed preference approaches to estimate the economic value of environmental remediation in Great Lakes Areas of Concern.

Ecosystem Services, Recreation, and Environmental Valuation

My research also examines the economic value of ecosystem services, environmental amenities, and outdoor recreation. This work includes applications of stated preference and nonmarket valuation methods to understand how individuals value environmental resources and recreational opportunities.
